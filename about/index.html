About Bellmore Water Damage Pros

We run a 24/7 dispatch line for water damage in Bellmore, NY and nearby South Shore towns. This page explains how a call works, what we do, and what we do not.

Bellmore Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration service for homes and businesses in Bellmore, NY, ZIP 11710, and the South Shore towns around it. You call one number, describe what you see and licensed and insured restoration professionals are sent to the address. The line is answered 24 hours a day.

We keep this page plain on purpose. There are no staff biographies, no invented review counts and no certification claims, because you should be able to trust what a restoration company says before it enters your home.

What happens when you call

  1. 1

    You describe the loss

    Where the water is, where it came from, and whether you can safely shut it off.

  2. 2

    You get next steps

    We tell you what to do while you wait, including when to stop and call 911 or your utility.

  3. 3

    A crew is sent

    Same-day response is offered when available. We never promise an exact arrival time.

  4. 4

    Scope and price first

    The scope and price are explained before work starts, and you decide what goes ahead.

What we do

We handle the water damage itself: stopping the spread, extracting standing water, drying the structure and coordinating repairs. The full list is on our services page, grouped by cause, from burst pipes to flood damage to commercial losses.

We work with moisture meters, thermal cameras, extraction units, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and we record moisture readings so the end of a job is measured and not guessed. Mold is a secondary service, covered at mold remediation, and it follows water that was not dried in time.

What we do not do

We do not decide what your insurance covers. Your insurer does, and the wording of your policy decides. We can document the damage with photos, readings and a written scope so you have what an insurer typically asks for.

We are not a waterproofing or foundation company. If a basement takes on water every time it rains, restoration dries and cleans it, but fixing the cause belongs to a drainage or foundation trade. We tell homeowners that directly.

We do not promise arrival times, claim approvals or specific outcomes. We explain what a job involves, and you decide.

The standards we point to

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published standard for mold remediation. You can ask any company you call, including us, whether its crews work to them. A company that cannot answer that question plainly is worth another call.

How a restoration job is measured

A dry-looking wall can hold water. Crews use a moisture meter to read the material itself and a thermal camera to find cool, wet areas behind the surface. Those readings are written down on the first visit and again at each follow-up, so the job ends when the numbers say it should and not when the surface feels dry.

Equipment is chosen for the loss. Extraction units pull standing water, air movers push air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into the structure. How many units a room needs depends on its size, the materials and how wet it is, which is why the scope is written after measuring.

Why we explain scope and price first

Water damage is stressful, and a vague promise is the last thing a stressed homeowner needs. We explain what the work involves and what it costs before it starts, so you can compare, call your insurer and decide. The cost factors for each type of loss are laid out on the service pages without invented numbers.

If a job is bigger than it first looked, for example when a wall opened for inspection shows more moisture than the readings suggested, the change is explained before more work is done. That is the practical meaning of scope and price explained before work starts.

What a call does not commit you to

Calling the line is a conversation, not a contract. You can ask what a crew would do, what the scope might include and how drying is measured, and then decide. If the loss turns out to be small, we say so, and if it belongs with a plumber or a drainage contractor, we tell you that too.

Keep your insurer in the loop from the start. Ask what your policy covers, keep the photos and readings, and save receipts for anything you buy or pay for during the emergency.
