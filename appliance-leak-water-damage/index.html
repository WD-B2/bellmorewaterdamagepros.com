Appliance leak water damage is the wet flooring, cabinets and walls left behind when a washer, dishwasher, refrigerator line or similar appliance leaks. If an appliance is leaking in your Bellmore home now, close its supply valve, unplug it only if the floor around the outlet is dry, and call our 24/7 line.

Water and cords do not mix. If water is touching an outlet, a power strip or the appliance plug, stay out and cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable. For any electrical or gas risk, call 911 or your utility first. Then call dispatch, where same-day response is offered when available.

When an appliance floods the floor

A pressurized supply hose behaves like a burst pipe. It releases water as long as the valve is open, and a washer hose can empty hundreds of gallons into a house while no one is home. Treat any running leak as an emergency and close the valve first.

A slow leak is quieter but still harmful. Water behind a dishwasher or under a refrigerator can soak the subfloor and the back of the cabinet for weeks, and it often shows up only as a soft floor or a musty smell.

Washer, dishwasher and refrigerator: three common failures

Washing machine

Rubber supply hoses age, and a burst hose or loose coupling floods the laundry area. Drain hose clogs can overflow the standpipe.

Dishwasher

A failed inlet valve, loose clamp or cracked door seal can leak into the cabinet base and the floor.

Refrigerator and ice maker

A small supply line to the ice maker can kink or crack and drip behind the unit for a long time.

Other units

Humidifiers, utility sinks and portable dehumidifiers can overflow or leak and wet the floor.

What to do in the first minutes

  • Close the supply valve, usually behind or beside the appliance, and shut off the main if you cannot find it.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the floor around the outlet is dry. Otherwise leave it and cut power at the panel if it is dry and reachable.
  • Pull the appliance away from the wall if it is safe, so water and air can reach the area behind it.
  • Photograph the appliance, the hose and the water spread before cleanup.
  • Keep the failed hose or part, because your insurer may ask to see it.

How we handle an appliance leak

We find where the water went, which is often farther than the puddle suggests. Water follows the cabinet base, floor seams and wall bottoms. A moisture meter and thermal camera map it, and extraction removes standing water before air movers and dehumidifiers dry the area.

The floor is the next question. If the leak was in a kitchen, cabinets and flooring may both be affected, and kitchen water damage restoration explains those decisions. If the leak soaked wood flooring, hardwood floor water damage repair describes cupping and drying.
