Emergency Basement Water Damage in Bellmore, NY

A wet basement gets worse by the hour, because water pulls moisture into walls, stored boxes and framing. Our 24/7 line is open for Bellmore basement emergencies, and the first job is making the room safe.

Basement water damage is standing or seeping water in a below-grade room, and the finished walls, flooring and stored contents it soaks. It usually starts from a sump failure, groundwater seepage, a sewer or drain backup or a leak from above, and each source needs a different response.

If water is in the basement, stay out when it reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, and call 911 or your utility for any electrical or gas concern. Our line is open 24/7, so call when it is safe. While you wait, move valuables upstairs, turn off the sump discharge only if you can reach it dry and photograph the water line.

When a wet basement is an emergency

It is an emergency when water is deeper than an inch or two, when it is still rising, when it reaches outlets or the furnace or when it came from a drain backup. Below-grade rooms trap water, so it does not drain away on its own the way it might from a garage.

It is also an emergency at night. A sump that fails at 3 a.m. can leave inches of water by morning. After-hours calls get the same response as daytime ones, and same-day response is offered when available.

Finished basements, contents and what gets saved

Finished basements are the hardest to dry, because drywall, insulation, carpet and framing all absorb water. The first hours decide how much can be saved.

Drywall and insulation

Wet drywall often needs flood cuts, and soaked insulation usually has to come out.

Carpet and flooring

Carpet and pad are checked first. See carpet water damage for the decision steps.

Stored boxes and furniture

Cardboard and upholstered pieces absorb water quickly and are moved first.

Appliances and utilities

Furnaces, water heaters and electrical gear are left to the proper trades to inspect.

How we extract and dry a basement

Extraction units remove standing water first. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the room and the walls. For deep or large jobs, water extraction explains the pumps and wet vacuums, and structural drying covers moisture mapping.

We take readings on each visit and leave equipment in place until the numbers read normal. If sewage or floodwater is involved, sewage backup cleanup covers the safety steps first.

Safety before anything else

If your basement is flooded

  • Do not enter if water is near outlets, the panel or the furnace.
  • Call 911 or your utility for electrical hazards or a gas smell.
  • Keep children and pets away from the water, especially if it came from a drain.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Photograph the water line and damaged items before they are moved.

After the water is out

Once standing water is gone, the room still holds moisture in the slab, the wall bases and anything stored on the floor. A basement that smells musty a week later usually dried too fast on top and stayed damp underneath.

We keep dehumidifiers running and take readings until the walls and floor read normal. If the water came from a drain or the ground, we also explain which items are cleaned and which are thrown away.
