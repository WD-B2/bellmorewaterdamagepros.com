How to Find and Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Bellmore, NY

The best time to learn where your main shutoff is happens on a dry afternoon. This guide shows how to find it, test it and label it before you need it.

To shut off the water main in a Bellmore home, find the valve where the water line enters the house, usually in a basement, utility room or crawl space near the meter. Turn a round wheel handle clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever handle a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe. Then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the line.

Most people never touch this valve until water is already on the floor. A valve that has not moved in years can stick, and finding it in a panic costs minutes you do not have. Ten minutes of practice now means you can stop the flow quickly and then deal with the problem.

Where the main shutoff usually is

The main shutoff sits on the pipe that brings water into the house, usually before it branches to the rest of the plumbing. In houses with basements, look on the wall facing the street, near where a pipe comes through the foundation. In houses on a slab or with a crawl space, look in a utility closet, a garage wall or near the water heater.

There are often two valves. One is inside the house and belongs to you. The other is outside, near the curb or the meter pit, and usually belongs to the water supplier. Do not operate the outside valve unless your supplier tells you to.

Who supplies your water in Bellmore

Your water bill names your supplier, and that is the first place to look if you are unsure who to call. The Town of Hempstead Water Department lists six districts on its own website, and Bellmore is not among them. The page says all other communities in the Town are served by private companies or villages.

That matters in practice. If you cannot find the outside valve, or you need the supplier to shut off water at the street, the number on your bill is the one that will be answered. Save it in your phone now with the label water supplier.

Two valve types and how each turns

Valve typeWhat it looks likeHow to close it
Gate valveRound wheel handle, like an outdoor faucetTurn clockwise several full turns until it stops
Ball valveShort lever handleTurn a quarter turn so the lever sits across the pipe
Meter valveSmall valve beside the water meterClose it the same way as the type it matches, if you can reach it

Gate valves are common in older homes and can wear or corrode over time. Ball valves are common in newer work and are easier to operate, but they can still stick if never used.

How to test the valve twice a year

A five-minute test

  • Find the valve and clear anything stored in front of it.
  • Close it slowly and fully, then open a faucet at the lowest level to confirm the flow drops.
  • Check the valve and the pipe for drips while it is closed.
  • Open the valve fully, then back it off a quarter turn so it does not seize in the open position.
  • Run a faucet to confirm water is back and the pressure is normal.

Label it so anyone can use it

Put a tag on the valve that says main water shutoff, with the direction to close it. Take a photo of the valve and its location and keep it with your insurance papers. If you have a lever valve, note which direction is closed so a family member or a house sitter does not guess.

Show every adult in the house where it is. If you rent, ask the landlord for the location and who to call after hours.

If the valve is stuck or leaking

Never force a valve with a pipe wrench or a long bar. A gate valve stem can snap, and a forced fitting can turn a slow drip into a flood. If it will not move with firm hand pressure, stop, and call your supplier or a licensed plumber for the next step.

If a valve is dripping at the stem when it is closed, that is a sign it needs service. A plumber can replace it, and doing that on a calm day is far easier than during an emergency. If a pipe has already burst, close any other valve you can reach, and see our page on burst pipe water damage for what to do next.

Why this matters in Bellmore

Bellmore homes sit in a hamlet where houses of different ages share the same block, so the valve type and location vary from house to house. Walk your own house rather than assuming it matches a neighbor. If you live south of Merrick Road, where low ground has taken on water in past storms, keep the valve clear of stored boxes so you can reach it even when the floor is wet.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Hempstead Building Department before replacing a valve or any section of supply line. The department enforces plumbing codes, and a licensed plumber will know when a permit applies. Keep the paperwork if you have work done.

After you shut it off

Once the water is off, drain the line by opening faucets, and turn off the water heater if the tank may have lost water. Photograph the damage before moving anything, and call your insurer. If water has spread across a floor, a crew can start with water extraction, and the 24/7 line is open any hour.
