Commercial water damage restoration is the extraction, drying and cleanup of water in shops, offices, restaurants and multi-unit buildings, with a focus on limiting downtime. A pipe failure at closing time or a roof leak over a stock room is an emergency, because the stock and the schedule are both at risk. Our dispatch line answers 24/7.

While you wait, shut off the water supply, cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry, and move inventory and records off the floor.

Commercial spaces add questions a house does not. Who holds the lease, who controls the building systems and who needs to be notified before a crew enters? Having those answers ready speeds up the call.

When a business water loss is an emergency

Treat it as an emergency if water is moving toward stock, electrical equipment, records or a neighbor in a shared building. A roof leak over a dining room, a sprinkler or supply line failure and a toilet overflow in a multi-unit property can all spread through floors and walls before staff arrive in the morning.

After-hours calls are normal in this work. Nights, weekends and holidays are when many commercial failures are discovered, and same-day response is offered when available. We cannot promise an exact arrival, but we will walk you through what to protect first.

What to protect before the crew arrives

Immediate steps for owners and managers

  • Close the water supply to the unit or building, and tell tenants and neighbors in a shared building.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. Otherwise call the utility.
  • Move inventory, records and electronics off the floor and away from the wet area.
  • Photograph the damage, the source and affected stock before anything is moved.
  • Write down who has keys, alarm codes and building access, and who needs to be reached after hours.

The Merrick Road corridor and keeping the doors open

Merrick Road is the main commercial corridor in Bellmore, and our own address is on it. Businesses along it share walls, roofs and plumbing with neighbors, so one failure can reach several tenants.

The practical lesson is to plan for water before it arrives. Know where your main shutoff is, keep records and inventory lists in a form you can reach, and ask your insurer whether your commercial policy covers flood, because a typical homeowners policy does not and business policies vary.

How we limit downtime

The aim is to dry the space while keeping as much of it usable as possible. We extract water, map moisture across floors and walls and contain wet areas so dry areas can stay open. Equipment placement is planned around traffic and safety.

Larger spaces use the same equipment as a house in greater numbers, including air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and moisture meters, with the drying approach laid out on our structural drying page. If the loss started with a burst line, the steps in water extraction explain how standing water is removed first.

Landlords, tenants and shared buildings

In multi-unit buildings, the source is often one unit and the damage is several. We document where the water started, which units and common areas were affected and what was removed, so those responsible and their insurers have a clear record.

We do not decide who pays. That is for the lease, the policies and the parties involved. What we do is give a neutral written scope, readings and photos that can be shared, and we ask you who needs access and when.
