Crawl Space Water Damage in Bellmore, NY

The void under your floor holds water longer than any other part of a house, and nobody looks at it. When something goes wrong there, it often stays wrong for weeks.

Crawl space water damage is the wet soil, insulation, joists and subfloor that result when water gets into the low void beneath a raised floor. It can come from a pipe, a storm, groundwater rising or a vapor problem that has been quietly building. The space is dark, tight and poorly ventilated, so drying it takes planning rather than speed alone.

If you have a crawl space, you may notice cold floors, a damp smell in the rooms above or bounce in the floorboards before you ever see water. A pipe leaking under the house is a good reason to shut off the main and then call. Stay out of a crawl space that has standing water near wiring, and never enter one alone.

What water does in an under-floor void

Floor joists, subfloor and insulation sit close to the ground, which means they are close to the water. Wood absorbs from the underside, swells and loses strength, and it dries slowly because air barely moves.

Moisture also rises as vapor from damp soil, wets insulation from below and condenses on cold ducts and pipes. A crawl space does not need a flood to cause trouble. A small leak or a few weeks of wet soil is enough to affect the floor above.

Four ways water gets into a crawl space

Plumbing leaks

A supply or drain line fails and water pools on the ground or runs down joists. A supply break is fastest and is covered in burst pipe water damage.

Storm and surface water

Heavy rain pushes water through vents, door openings and wall seams, especially when drainage slopes toward the house.

Rising groundwater

After heavy rain, the water table can lift and seep into the space from below.

Vapor and condensation

Damp soil and warm air meeting cold surfaces wet insulation and wood without any visible puddle.

Signs the space below is wet

The most reliable clue is a change in the rooms above. Floors feel colder, doors stick, boards cup or sag and a damp smell shows up near registers and vents. Pests and rust on exposed metal are other signs that moisture has been in the space for a while.

If you can see into the space from the access hatch, look for dark soil, hanging insulation, white staining on masonry and any visible water line on piers or walls. Photograph it from the opening and leave the entry for the crew.

How a crawl space is dried

The work is slower than a room, and it is built around access. We start by shutting off the source, then extract any standing water with pumps and extraction units. A moisture meter shows how wet the joists and subfloor are, and a thermal camera helps spot wet insulation without crawling everywhere.

Wet insulation is removed because it holds water against the wood above it. Then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until the readings are normal. For floors above a wet space, the process connects to structural drying, since the subfloor and the framing are what we are drying.

After a big storm: why south of Merrick Road should check below the floor

South of Merrick Road, press reports from Hurricane Sandy in 2012 describe several feet of water in parts of South Bellmore, and the National Weather Service records Hurricane Irene water up to 4 feet deep in Bellmore in August 2011. We are not saying every home has a crawl space.

If yours does, a storm like that is a reason to open the access hatch within a day or two of the water leaving. Check your address on the FEMA flood map, and ask your insurer whether flood is covered, because a typical homeowners policy does not cover flood damage.
