A dense hamlet, and what that means for water damage

The 2020 Census counted 20,583 people and 6,866 housing units in North Bellmore, across about 2.6 square miles of land and no water area. That works out to roughly 2,600 housing units per square mile, so many homes sit close together and a leak in one is often a concern for the next.

Census counts do not tell us how many homes have basements or what the pipes are made of, so we do not guess. What the count does show is a large residential community with five elementary schools run by the North Bellmore school district, among them Martin Avenue and Saw Mill Road, and plenty of ordinary household water sources.

Where North Bellmore water damage usually starts

Without a documented flood pattern, the useful question is where water can start inside a house. Supply lines, washing machine hoses, dishwashers, water heaters and air conditioner drain lines all fail, and a failure can run for hours or days before anyone sees a stain.

The pages on burst pipes, water heater leaks and AC leak damage cover the cause, and wall and drywall damage explains what happens once water gets behind a surface.

Heavy rain, roofs and ceilings

Rain gets in through roofs, flashing, windows and siding, and the first sign is often a ceiling stain after the storm has passed. A wet ceiling can hold water above the paint, so do not poke it, and keep a bucket under any drip. Ceiling water damage repair and storm water damage cleanup explain how a crew finds the source and dries the space.

If water fell through a light fixture or you can see sagging, keep clear and call. Do not use the room until the electrical hazard is assessed.

If water is spreading: before we arrive

Stop the source

Close the main valve or the valve at the fixture. If you cannot find it, call the line and we will walk you through it.

Check the power

Turn off power to a wet area only from a dry panel. If water has reached the panel or outlets, leave and call 911 or the utility.

Move people out of the wet room

Keep children and pets away from standing water, which can be electrified or dirty.

Lift valuables

Raise papers, electronics and small items onto dry furniture if you can do it safely.

Photograph before cleanup

Take photos of the damage and the source before anything is moved. Your insurer will likely want them.
