Where South Bellmore sits

Bellmore is a hamlet and census-designated place in the Town of Hempstead, on the South Shore of Nassau County and about 6 miles north of Jones Beach State Park. The 2020 Census counted 16,297 people and 5,776 housing units in about 3.0 square miles, and roughly half a square mile of that area is water.

That mix of close-set streets and nearby bays is why South Bellmore water calls look different from calls in the northern part of town. Merrick Road runs east to west through the hamlet, with Sunrise Highway and the Southern State Parkway farther north, and the streets south of Merrick Road are closest to the tidal water that drains toward the bays.

Storm drains, tides and what comes back up

The Herald reported in 2019 that the storm surge from Hurricane Sandy, about 11 feet, forced saltwater into storm drains here. The newspaper said the drains discharge toward the bays to the south, so a high tide or a surge can back water up onto streets.

The Town planned road raising of at least four feet, new drainage and check valves on Shore Road, with New York State funding set aside for flood work in Bellmore and Merrick. Those timelines date from 2019, so ask the Town where the work stands today.

The practical point for a homeowner is what that water is. Water that comes back through drains or in from the street carries sewage and debris, so it counts as contaminated. Do not wade in, do not run a household vacuum on it and do not rely on fans. The pages on sewage backup cleanup and basement water damage cleanup explain what a crew removes and why porous materials often cannot be saved.

Water damage that has nothing to do with a storm

Many South Bellmore calls start indoors. A supply line splits under a sink, a washer hose lets go, a water heater tank fails or an upstairs pipe drips for days before anyone sees a stain. These losses are usually clean water at first, which is easier to dry, but they spread fast through drywall, subfloors and insulation.

We find the extent with moisture meters and a thermal camera, then remove standing water with water extraction equipment and dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. See burst pipe water damage and appliance leak water damage for the steps in each case, and structural drying for how readings decide when equipment leaves.

If water is coming in now: before we arrive

Close the supply

If a pipe or appliance is the source, shut the nearest valve or the main. If the water is coming from outside, do not go out to look for the cause.

Stay out of standing water

Water that reached outlets or the panel can be live. If you see sparks, smell gas or cannot reach the panel dry, leave and call 911 or the utility.

Treat drain water as contaminated

Water that rose through a floor drain or toilet should not be touched with bare hands. Keep children and pets out.

Raise what is safe to lift

Move papers, electronics and small items off the floor if you can do it without stepping into the water.

Take photos first

Photograph the water line, the source and damaged items before anything is moved, because your insurer will likely ask.

Flood coverage and your policy

The New York State Department of Financial Services says a typical homeowners or renter policy does not cover flood damage, and a separate policy through the National Flood Insurance Program may. Some policies cover water back-up or sewage damage by endorsement, but that coverage is often excluded when the back-up comes from a flood.

Read your own policy and ask your insurer, and report the loss as soon as possible. Keep photos, moisture readings and receipts, and do not throw away damaged items until the insurer says you can. We document what we find, but your insurer decides what is covered.
