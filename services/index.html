Most people searching for help do not know the trade name for what they need. They know a ceiling is stained, a basement is wet or a washer hose let go. That is why this index is organized by cause and by the material affected, not alphabetically.

Every page below follows the same basic path. We stop or identify the source, remove standing water, map where the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, and explain scope and price before work starts. For the full overview, start with our water damage restoration in Bellmore page.

Pipes and plumbing

When pressurized water is the source, speed matters because the leak does not stop on its own. See burst pipe water damage for supply line failures and frozen pipe damage for thawed ice splits.

A failed tank is a different problem than a failed pipe, so water heater leaks have their own steps. Water that rises through the floor from a line under the concrete is covered under slab leaks.

Appliances and HVAC

Appliance failures are slow and sneaky. Washer, dishwasher and refrigerator leaks often run for days behind a cabinet or under a floor before anyone sees a stain.

The condensate line on an air conditioner is another quiet source, covered in AC leak water damage, and the sink and cabinet zone has its own page on kitchen water damage.

Weather and building envelope

Where the water enters the building decides the response. Storm water damage covers wind-driven rain through the envelope, and ceiling water damage covers stains, sagging and roof leaks.

Rising water is a separate problem. Use flood damage restoration for coastal and surface flooding, basement water damage cleanup for below-grade rooms and crawl space water damage for the void under a floor.

Water removal and drying

Standing water comes out first, which is what water extraction covers. Drying comes next, and structural drying and dehumidification explains moisture mapping, equipment and how readings decide when a job is done.

Contaminated water is handled differently from clean water, so sewage backup cleanup starts with safety and a clear list of what cannot be saved.

Floors and walls

The material you are looking at decides the repair. Hardwood and laminate floors can cup or swell, while wet carpet depends on the pad and how long it stayed wet.

Walls hide most of the damage. Wall and drywall water damage explains baseboards, insulation and when a section has to be cut out and replaced.

Commercial properties

Shops, offices and multi-unit buildings lose income while they are wet. Commercial water damage restoration is built around access, documentation and keeping downtime short.

Along Merrick Road and the other commercial strips in Bellmore, a tenant, a landlord and an insurer may all need paperwork from the same loss.

Mold is a secondary service, not a first response

Mold needs moisture, so it follows water damage that was not dried in time or that came from a leak nobody found. If you already see dark spots, smell a musty odor or have a wall that stayed damp after a repair, read mold remediation in Bellmore. It explains testing, containment and independent clearance, and it follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard as the published reference.

If the water event happened in the last few days, call first. Fast drying is the cheapest mold prevention, and the water damage pages above come before this one for a reason.

How to choose the right page

Start with the source if you know it. A running pipe, a failed appliance and a storm each point to a different page. If you do not know the source, start with where the water is. A wet ceiling, a wet basement and a wet kitchen cabinet each have a page because each needs a different inspection and different tools.

If your situation fits two pages, call the line and we will sort it out. The cause and the material often overlap, and the crew that arrives will measure moisture across all of it. ZIP 11710 and the neighboring towns on our service area page get the same 24/7 dispatch.

Cost depends on the size of the affected area, the category of water, the materials involved and how long they were wet. Each service page includes a table of those factors without invented numbers, because a fair scope can only be written after the crew measures moisture on site.

Insurance works the same way. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded. Check your policy and ask your insurer before you decide, and keep every photo and receipt.

What to do in the first hour, whatever the cause

Some steps are the same for every kind of water loss. Stop the source if you can do so safely, which usually means closing the main shutoff valve. Keep everyone out of standing water if it may have reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, and call 911 or your utility if you are unsure.

Take photos and short videos of the water, the damaged items and the source before anything is moved. New York State Department of Financial Services guidance says to document your losses before cleaning anything up, and to contact your insurer as soon as possible. Then call the line, because hours matter more than days when materials are wet.

Why the cause changes the work

Clean water from a supply line can often be dried in place, while water from a drain, a sewer or a flood is treated as contaminated and porous materials may have to be removed. That is the reason this index is split by cause. A burst pipe and a sewage backup can soak the same carpet and need very different handling.

Time matters too. Water that sat for a day or two behaves differently from water caught on the first day, and the crew checks both what got wet and how long it stayed wet. The standard that restoration professionals point to is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.
