Emergency Storm Water Damage in Bellmore, NY

When a storm pushes rain through the walls and window frames of your house, the damage is already moving into the framing. Our 24/7 line is open for Bellmore storm water emergencies, and we start by finding where the rain got in.

Storm water damage is the wetting of a building by rain, wind and debris that breach the envelope, meaning the roof edges, siding, windows, doors, vents and gutters that are supposed to keep weather out. It is different from rising flood water, and it is different from a single roof leak, because storm rain usually enters in several places at once.

If the storm is still going, stay clear of wet ceilings, move people away from bulging drywall and keep out of rooms where water has reached outlets or the panel. Our line is open 24/7, so call when it is safe to do so. While you wait, move valuables off the floor, put buckets under active drips and photograph every wet area before anything is moved.

When storm water is an emergency

Treat it as an emergency when water is running down walls, when a ceiling is bowing, when insulation is soaked or when rain is coming in faster than you can catch it. Those are the conditions where drywall softens in hours and framing stays wet for days.

It is also an emergency when the storm hits at night. Water that enters at 2 a.m. and is found at 7 a.m. has had five hours to travel. Same-day response is offered when available, and after-hours calls get the same attention as daytime ones.

How storm water gets into a Bellmore house

Wind changes the direction of rain, so water that never bothers a house on a calm day gets pushed sideways into gaps. The common entry points are listed below, and a crew checks all of them rather than only the room where you saw the stain.

Window and door frames

Wind-driven rain forces water past old caulk and worn weatherstripping, then runs down inside the wall.

Roof edges and flashing

Lifted shingles and loose flashing at chimneys and dormers let water under the roof covering.

Siding, vents and trim

Gaps behind siding, bath fans and dryer vents give water a path into the wall cavity.

Gutters and downspouts

Overflow at a clogged gutter runs behind the fascia and down the exterior wall.

What Bellmore storms have shown homeowners

The National Weather Service records that Hurricane Irene in August 2011 put water up to 4 feet deep in Bellmore, and local press reported about five inches of rain in six hours in 2014. Newbridge Road at Sunrise Highway was among the spots that flooded then. The practical lesson is that a storm can bring rain from above and water from below in the same night, and each needs its own response.

Linden Street flooded from Newbridge Creek in 2020. If your wet floor is near a creek or low street after a storm like that, the water may be flood water rather than roof water, and it is worth saying so on the call.

While you wait for the crew

Safe steps during and after the storm

  • Keep people away from sagging ceilings and wet outlets, and call 911 or your utility for downed wires or a gas smell.
  • Place buckets and towels under active drips, and lift rugs and electronics off wet floors.
  • Take dated photos of every wet area, the storm damage outside and any damaged contents.
  • Do not climb onto a wet roof or tarp it yourself in wind. Leave roof access to the crew.
  • Keep receipts for emergency supplies, because your insurer may ask for them.

Ask about the drying plan before you agree

A good storm job has a written scope that names each wet room, the equipment going in and how many days it is expected to run. Ask what readings will tell the crew the job is finished, because the answer should be numbers and not an opinion.

Also ask what happens if more water shows up behind a wall once drying starts. Storm leaks often hide a second entry point, and it is better to know in advance how added scope is handled.

Insurance often responds to sudden and accidental storm damage, and a flood policy is usually a separate policy. Wear and tear or a roof left in poor repair is often treated differently. After the drying: repairs and what to watch for

Once readings are normal, wet drywall, baseboards and insulation are repaired or replaced. Wall and drywall repair covers that stage, and structural drying explains how the equipment and readings work.

Watch for musty smells and dark spots in the weeks after a storm. They usually mean something stayed damp, and the fix starts with drying the source rather than covering the stain.
