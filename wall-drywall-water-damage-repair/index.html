Wall and drywall water damage is moisture in the paper-faced gypsum board, baseboards, insulation and framing of an interior or exterior wall. Drywall wicks water upward and outward, so the stain you see is often smaller than the wet area behind it.

If a wall is wet now, stop the source, move furniture away and call the 24/7 dispatch line. Do not press on a bulging wall, and keep outlets in wet walls switched off until the area is checked.

Reading a wet wall

A wall shows damage in stages. Paint blisters and bubbles first, then a brown ring forms, baseboards swell and drywall softens. By the time drywall crumbles, moisture has usually been present for days.

A moisture meter and a thermal camera show the wet area without cutting. That keeps repairs small, because we open only the sections that read wet.

Flood cuts: when and how high

A flood cut removes drywall at a set height above the wettest point so the cavity can dry. Contractors usually cut a bit higher than the visible line, because water wicks upward through the board.

We cut when insulation is soaked, when the water was contaminated or when readings stay high after drying. A neat cut along a stud line makes repair easier, and a taped horizontal edge keeps the patch small.

Baseboards, insulation and paint

Baseboards are the first thing to swell, and they are often removed so the wall base can dry. Insulation is the sponge inside the wall. Wet fiberglass can sometimes dry in place, but wet cellulose or foam usually has to come out.

Paint is the last step. Sealing or painting a wall before it is dry traps moisture, so we recommend waiting for stable readings. If the wet area is on a ceiling, ceiling water damage repair is the page that applies.

How a wall gets dried

The process is simple to describe and strict in practice.

  1. 1

    Find the source

    Stop the leak or the storm entry before any drying starts.

  2. 2

    Open as little as needed

    Small cuts or removed baseboards let air reach the cavity.

  3. 3

    Dry the cavity

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run, and readings are logged.

  4. 4

    Repair and finish

    Drywall, tape, texture and paint go back after readings are normal.

Outlets, switches and wiring in a wet wall

Wet walls often contain outlets and switches. Keep those circuits off until the wall is checked, and have a licensed electrician inspect anything that got wet before it is used again.

We dry the cavity around the wiring and leave the electrical work to the right trade. Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Hempstead Building Department when wiring is part of the repair.

Exterior walls and siding

An exterior wall can be wet from outside even when the inside looks fine. Failed caulk, loose siding and window flashing let rain in behind the finish, and the water sits against the sheathing.

When that is the cause, repairing the drywall alone will not last. The entry point has to be sealed, and the cavity dried first.

Walls after storms and plumbing leaks

Wind-driven rain can leave a wall wet from the outside in. Storm water damage explains how entry points are traced. If water came from a supply line, structural drying covers the equipment used inside the cavity.

Wet wall safety checks before anyone cuts or sands

Do this first

  • Stay away from outlets and switches on a wet wall and turn off that circuit only from a dry panel
  • Do not press on a bulging ceiling or wall, because trapped water can release suddenly
  • Open windows and run a fan if the room is not otherwise affected
  • Keep dust down and do not sand or scrape older painted surfaces before asking a professional
  • Photograph the wall, the stain edge and the source

The standard behind the work

The published reference for water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500, and for mold work it is ANSI/IICRC S520. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to them.
